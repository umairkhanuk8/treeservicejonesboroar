Storm Damage

Common Tree Problems Caused by Storms in Northeast Arkansas

📅 July 2025 ⏱ 5 min read 📍 Northeast Arkansas
Quick Answer The most common storm-related tree problems in Northeast Arkansas include hanging broken limbs, partially uprooted trees, split trunk crotches, and trees that have fallen against fences or structures. Saturated soil from heavy rain makes uprooting more likely, and straight-line winds frequently break large canopy limbs without toppling the whole tree.

Jonesboro and surrounding Craighead County see their share of severe weather. Thunderstorm lines, straight-line wind events, and occasional ice storms all affect the area's mature tree population in predictable ways. Knowing what to look for after a storm helps you prioritize what needs immediate attention.

Hanging and Broken Limbs

This is the most immediate hazard after most Northeast Arkansas storm events. High winds break large limbs that remain suspended in the canopy, caught by surrounding branches or held by a strip of bark. These are called widow-makers because they can drop without warning. Keep everyone away from under any affected tree until a crew can remove them safely.

Split Trunk Crotches

Many mature trees in established Jonesboro neighborhoods have co-dominant stems growing from the same point. This structure is visually appealing but mechanically weak. Under wind or ice load, these crotches split, tearing the trunk vertically. A partially split crotch is not stable—the remaining wood holding the two halves together can give way at any point. Expert tree removal support is typically the right response for a major trunk split.

Partially Uprooted Trees

Heavy rain saturates soil across Craighead County, reducing the grip root systems have in the ground. When strong winds follow saturated ground, trees that would otherwise hold can shift or tip. A partially uprooted tree still under load is extremely unpredictable. These need to be evaluated and removed by a crew with the equipment to handle controlled removal under tension. Rapid storm-damage response is the right call for any partially uprooted tree near a structure.

Trees Down Across Fences and Driveways

Fallen trees crossing driveways or resting on fencing are common post-storm situations. If the fallen tree is on the ground with no tension and clear of utility lines, this is cleanup work. If it is resting against a fence under load, the fence is holding weight, and removal must be done carefully. Property cleanup following a storm is often prioritized in phases: active hazards first, blocked access next, then general debris.

Ice Storm Damage

Late-winter ice events produce a different pattern. Ice accumulation adds weight to every branch, and limbs that would not fail under wind break under the compressive weight. Bradford pears, common throughout Jonesboro neighborhoods, are particularly prone to splitting under ice load due to their naturally weak branch structure.

Post-Storm Hazard Assessment Checklist

After any significant storm, walk your property carefully before letting children or pets outside. Look for:

  • Limbs that are partially attached and hanging overhead
  • Trees that have shifted or developed a new lean
  • Bark torn downward on the trunk
  • Root flare movement or soil heaving on one side
  • Cracks in major crotches or branch unions
