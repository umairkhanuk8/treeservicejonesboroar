02. Tree Trimming & Professional Pruning

Pruning is not trimming for appearance alone. Structural pruning involves removing deadwood, correcting poor branch attachments, reducing overextended limbs, and improving crown balance without removing more live wood than the tree can recover from. Crown cleaning removes dead, crossing, and weakly attached branches. Crown reduction addresses limbs that have grown too close to roofs, driveways, or neighboring structures. We make proper pruning cuts at the correct location—not flush cuts or stubs that lead to decay and regrowth problems. We do not recommend or perform topping, which creates large wounds, accelerates decay, and produces weak, fast-growing sprouts.

04. Stump Grinding & Stump Removal

Stump grinding reduces a tree stump to wood chips below the soil surface. Standard grinding depths vary, and underground roots are typically left in place—grinding does not remove the entire root system. Surface roots extending from the stump can also be ground where equipment access allows. After grinding, the stump area can be filled with soil and prepared for seeding, sodding, or landscaping. We discuss depth requirements, debris removal preferences, and any underground utility considerations before starting stump work.

05. Hazardous, Dead & Declining Tree Assessment

Identifying a hazardous tree requires more than looking at it from the street. Signs of concern include visible decay or cavities at the trunk base, fungal fruiting bodies on roots or bark, significant lean that wasn't present before, root-plate movement after heavy rain, cracked or included bark at branch unions, dead tops or large areas of crown dieback, and hanging or partially attached limbs. Not every defect means removal is necessary—the appropriate response depends on the defect type, its location, the tree's overall structure, and what's located within the potential fall zone. An on-site evaluation is required before making that determination.

Tree Health & Risk

Common Tree Problems in Jonesboro Properties & How to Recognize Them

Knowing what to look for helps property owners decide when to call for a professional assessment and when routine maintenance is sufficient.

Dead or Severely Declining Trees

A tree that has lost leaf-out in the majority of its canopy, shows extensive bark loss, has a soft or hollow trunk, or has advanced fungal fruiting at the base may be reaching the end of its structural lifespan. Dead trees don't fail on a predictable schedule—they can remain standing for years or drop limbs without warning. Assessment determines whether removal is appropriate based on the tree's condition and what's below it.

Bradford Pear Structural Failure

Bradford pears are common throughout Jonesboro neighborhoods and are known for developing included bark at branch unions—a weak attachment type where bark becomes trapped between stems rather than forming a strong wood connection. As the tree matures, these unions become increasingly likely to split during storms or under heavy leaf and ice loads. Removal or heavy reduction is often recommended before failure occurs.

Storm-Damaged Limbs & Hanging Wood

After a severe thunderstorm or ice event, limbs that appear attached may be partially broken and hanging under their own weight. Hanging wood is one of the more immediate concerns after a storm—it can drop without warning onto anything below. Assessment of the full canopy, not just the visible damage, is important after a major storm event.

Root Instability After Saturated Soil

Arkansas receives periods of heavy rainfall that saturate soil and reduce root-plate stability—especially in low-lying areas, near drainage channels, and on clay-heavy soils common in Craighead County. A tree that develops a new lean after significant rainfall, or shows signs of root-plate lifting on one side, warrants an evaluation before the next wind event.

Codominant Stems & Included Bark

Trees with two or more stems of roughly equal diameter growing from a common attachment point often develop included bark—a structurally weak union that may crack under load. The risk depends on the stem diameter, the attachment angle, what's in the fall zone, and whether supplemental support is a realistic option. Not every codominant stem is an immediate hazard, but many benefit from early structural pruning when trees are young.

Decay, Cavities & Fungal Growth

Decay inside a trunk or at branch unions is not always visible from the outside. Fungal fruiting bodies—shelf fungi, conks, or mushrooms—growing at the base or on the trunk surface often indicate internal decay. A cavity at or near the root flare, particularly on the side facing a structure or frequently used area, warrants a professional evaluation of the tree's remaining structural integrity.

Jonesboro Tree Service Projects & Generalized Case Studies

The following examples are generalized representations of the types of projects common to Jonesboro and Craighead County properties. These are not verified customer accounts.

The Valley View Residence — Storm-Damaged Tree Removal, Controlled Rigging & Property Protection

Situation: A large sweetgum, approximately 70 feet tall and 24 inches in diameter at breast height, sustained a major split at a codominant union during a severe thunderstorm. One stem had partially fallen and was leaning against the corner of the garage roof. The other stem remained standing but was structurally compromised.

Assessment: Both stems were evaluated for structural integrity and lean. The grounded stem was under significant tension against the structure. Two work zones were established—one at the front of the property and one along the fence line. Three parking spaces adjacent to the garage were temporarily marked off.

Work completed: The grounded stem was carefully rigged in sections and removed from the garage without additional roof damage. The standing stem was dismantled from the top down using a bucket truck and hand rigging. Eleven sections were lowered to the ground in controlled sequence. Debris was chipped and hauled the same day. The stump (approximately 22 inches at the grind point) was ground and the area cleaned up.

Result: Garage roof intact, fence protected, driveway and lawn usable the following day. Documentation provided to the homeowner.

The Red Wolf Boulevard Commercial Property — Tree Pruning, Parking-Lot Control & Phased Coordination

Situation: A commercial property with six mature oak trees lining the parking-lot entrance had multiple trees with branches extending over parking spaces, obstructing the entry signage, and dropping debris onto customer vehicles. One tree showed significant deadwood in the upper canopy. Work needed to be scheduled around business hours.

Assessment: Each of the six trees was individually evaluated. Four required crown cleaning and deadwood removal. Two required selective crown reduction to address limb extension over the parking area. One tree had a partially detached large limb requiring priority removal before the parking lot opened.

Work completed: Work was conducted over two early-morning sessions before business hours. The priority limb was removed during the first visit. Crown cleaning and reduction were completed on the remaining five trees during the second visit. Eight parking spaces were temporarily coned off per session. Debris was chipped and removed before the property opened each day. Documentation was provided to the property manager.

Result: Parking lot cleared of debris hazard, entry signage visible, six trees in improved structural condition. Zero disruption to business operations.

Common Questions

Tree Service FAQs for Jonesboro AR Property Owners

Straightforward answers to the questions property owners ask most often about tree removal, trimming, stump grinding, and emergency tree service in Jonesboro.

How much does tree removal cost in Jonesboro AR?
▼

Tree removal pricing in Jonesboro depends on the tree's height and trunk diameter, its structural condition, the available drop zone, access for equipment, the rigging method required, debris removal, and whether stump grinding is included. A small dead tree near an open lawn costs significantly less than a large oak between two structures in a narrow side yard requiring sectional dismantling and rigging. We provide written estimates after an on-site evaluation—not ballpark figures over the phone.

How do I know if a tree on my property is hazardous?
▼

Common warning signs include large dead branches in the canopy, visible cracks or splits at trunk unions, fungal growth at the base, a trunk that sounds hollow when knocked, significant new lean not present before recent storms, soil lifting or cracking near the root flare, and bark that's peeling or absent over large areas. That said, not all hazardous conditions are visible from the ground, and not all trees showing one of these signs are in immediate danger. An on-site evaluation provides a more accurate picture than photos or a description.

Is stump grinding included with tree removal?
▼

Stump grinding is typically quoted separately from tree removal. Some property owners want the stump ground immediately; others prefer to decide after seeing the stump or want to schedule grinding later. We discuss stump-grinding options as part of the estimate process so you can decide what works for your timeline and budget.

Does stump grinding remove the roots?
▼

Stump grinding reduces the visible stump to below grade—typically several inches below the soil surface. The underground root system remains in place and will decompose naturally over time. Surface roots extending from the stump can often be addressed during grinding where equipment can reach them. Grinding does not eliminate the possibility of root decay, soil settling over the stump area, or occasional sprout growth from remaining roots, depending on the tree species.

What should I do when a tree falls on my house or car?
▼

First, keep people away from the area—a tree that has partially fallen on a structure can shift unexpectedly, and a vehicle under a large limb should not be approached until the tree is secured. Contact us to assess the situation before any cutting begins. Trees under tension against a structure need to be rigged carefully to avoid uncontrolled movement during removal. If there's a potential utility line involved, contact your utility provider before any work begins. For insurance documentation purposes, photograph the scene before any debris is moved.

Can photos be used to get a tree removal estimate?
▼

Photos can help with initial planning and give us a general idea of the tree and property, but they're not a substitute for an on-site evaluation. Root conditions, trunk defects below the bark surface, soil saturation, underground utilities, equipment access restrictions, and the actual available drop zone typically can't be assessed from a photograph. We'll usually need to visit the property to provide an accurate, written estimate.

Why do Bradford pear trees fail so often in Jonesboro?
▼

Bradford pears develop included bark—a structurally weak attachment type where bark becomes trapped between co-equal stems as the tree grows. Unlike a healthy branch union where wood fibers interlock for strength, an included bark union is essentially two stems wedged together. As the trees mature and the crown grows heavier, these weak unions are increasingly likely to split under their own weight, wind load, or ice accumulation. Removal while the tree is still standing is generally safer and simpler than dealing with a split after the fact.

Do you serve commercial properties and apartment complexes?
▼

Yes. We work with commercial building owners, property management companies, HOAs, landlords, apartment managers, retail centers, offices, restaurants, warehouses, schools, churches, and developers throughout Jonesboro and Craighead County. Commercial work is scheduled around business operations, tenant access, and customer traffic. We provide documentation of work completed for property records.

Do you haul away the debris after tree removal?
▼

Debris removal—chipping brush, hauling log sections, and raking the work area—is included in our standard tree removal service. If you want to keep the wood for firewood or other purposes, let us know during the estimate process and we can discuss how to handle the material. Larger log sections can be cut to manageable lengths when requested.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Jonesboro?
▼

Permit requirements depend on the property location, the tree's size and species, and local ordinances that may apply. We recommend verifying with the City of Jonesboro or Craighead County before removing trees on certain property types. We can assist with the permit identification process and discuss what documentation may be needed, but we do not guarantee permit approval or act as a permitting agent.
