Lot Clearing and Land Clearing Services in Jonesboro and Craighead County

Land clearing involves more than cutting down trees. A lot ready for construction, a new fence line, a drainage project, or a landscaping redesign needs trees felled, stumps ground or removed, brush chipped or hauled, and the site left in a workable condition for whatever comes next. The scope of a clearing project depends on the size of the area, the density of trees and brush, what equipment can access the site, and what the intended next use of the land requires.

We work on land clearing projects ranging from single residential lots in established Jonesboro neighborhoods to larger tracts in unincorporated Craighead County. The approach on each depends on what the site actually looks like—tree density, species mix, stump size, soil conditions, drainage, and proximity to property boundaries or existing structures all affect how the work is planned and what equipment is used.

Selective Clearing vs. Full Lot Clearing

Full clearing removes all trees, brush, and stumps from the designated area. Selective clearing removes only specific trees—those in the building footprint, the driveway corridor, or the fence line—while preserving others that are part of the site plan. Both approaches are available, and the right choice depends on your project requirements and what you want the finished property to look like.

Common Land and Lot Clearing Applications

Land clearing in Jonesboro and the surrounding area is typically needed for one of several situations: a residential property where trees need to come out before a new construction project, a home addition, or a new driveway installation; a commercial development site where a tract of land needs to be prepared for a building pad, parking area, or access road; or a rural acreage property where overgrown brush and unwanted trees are limiting the usability of the land.

  • Residential lot clearing for new home construction or additions
  • Driveway and access route clearing on residential and rural properties
  • Commercial development site preparation
  • Fence line clearing for new fence installations
  • Rural acreage clearing for pasture, access, or property improvement
  • Brush and undergrowth removal on overgrown lots
  • Selective clearing to preserve specific trees within the project boundary

What the Clearing Process Involves

A clearing project begins with a walkthrough of the site. We confirm the clearing boundary, identify trees to be removed versus preserved in selective clearing projects, assess equipment access routes, note any underground or overhead utility considerations, and confirm how debris will be handled. From there, we provide a written estimate based on what the site actually requires.

On work days, trees are felled systematically across the clearing area. Brush goes through the chipper. Log sections are cut and staged for loading. Stumps are either ground in place using appropriate equipment or, where specified for construction projects, ground to a greater depth to reduce settling concerns. The site is cleaned of debris before the crew leaves, leaving it in a condition ready for the next phase of work.

For properties where trees need to be preserved—along a property boundary, in a buffer zone, or as part of the site plan—we mark those trees before work begins and make sure equipment and felling operations stay clear of their root zones. Felling trees near preserved trees requires care in planning the drop direction and rigging to avoid damage.

Equipment Used for Land Clearing

The equipment selected for a clearing project depends on the site. Chainsaws and chippers handle individual trees and brush in areas with restricted access. Skid steers and compact loaders move log sections and load debris on larger jobs. For clearing work in tight areas near structures or adjacent to property boundaries, hand work and climbing systems are used where equipment cannot reach or operate safely.

Stump grinding equipment is sized to match the stumps and site access conditions. Compact grinders fit through gates and work in residential backyards. Larger grinders handle substantial-diameter stumps on open commercial or rural sites more efficiently. We bring the right equipment combination for each project rather than relying on a single approach for every job.

Drainage, Erosion, and Site Considerations

Clearing vegetation changes how water moves across a property. On sloped lots or properties near drainage channels, removing trees and brush can increase surface runoff and erosion, particularly on bare soil areas after clearing. We flag these concerns during the site assessment so the property owner or contractor is aware and can plan soil stabilization, seeding, or drainage work appropriately after the clearing is complete.

We do not provide grading, excavation, or drainage construction services. Our scope is the tree and vegetation removal portion of the site work. If a clearing project is part of a larger construction project with a general contractor or site engineer, we coordinate the tree work around their schedule and site conditions as needed.

Land Clearing for Development Projects

Commercial development sites along Jonesboro's growing corridors—near Highway 49, Highway 63, and the Interstate 555 corridor—often require clearing before site work and construction begins. We work with builders, contractors, and developers to handle the tree and brush removal portion of site preparation on schedule and in a way that supports the broader project timeline.

For development projects where stump grinding services are needed as part of site preparation, we can address all stumps across the clearing area in the same project scope. For projects involving multiple phases, we coordinate with project managers to clear areas in sequence as they're needed, rather than clearing the entire site before it's ready for use. Our complete tree and vegetation management services cover the full clearing scope—trees, brush, and stumps—so you have one point of contact for the vegetation portion of the site prep work.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Land Clearing in Jonesboro

Does land clearing include stump grinding?
▼

Stump grinding can be included in the land clearing scope or priced separately depending on the project. For construction projects where the site needs to be as clear as possible below grade, including stump grinding in the clearing job makes the most sense. For clearing projects where the stumps will be left to decay or where the next phase of work will address them separately, they can be handled afterward. We discuss the full scope during the estimate so the price reflects what the project actually requires.

Can you clear a lot that has significant brush and undergrowth along with trees?
▼

Yes. Lots with a mix of mature trees, smaller volunteer trees, and brush undergrowth are handled as a combined clearing project. Brush and smaller material goes through the chipper; larger trees are felled, sectioned, and loaded. The density of brush affects the time required and is factored into the estimate after the site walkthrough. Dense brush growth that has established over several years takes more time per acre than a lot with mostly larger trees and minimal undergrowth.

Can some trees be preserved while others are cleared on the same lot?
▼

Yes — selective clearing is a straightforward part of most clearing projects. Trees to be preserved are identified and marked before work begins. We plan equipment movement and felling directions to avoid damage to preserved trees and their root zones. For construction projects where a site plan specifies tree preservation zones, we work within those boundaries and confirm the layout with you or the project manager before starting.

How is the cost of land clearing estimated?
▼

Land clearing estimates depend on the size of the area, the density and size of trees and brush, equipment access conditions, stump grinding requirements, debris disposal method, and any site-specific considerations like proximity to structures or property boundaries. A phone estimate without seeing the property is rarely accurate for clearing work. We provide written, site-specific estimates after walking the property and confirming the clearing scope with you.
